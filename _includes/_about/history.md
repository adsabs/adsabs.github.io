    <h2>From ADS to SciX</h2>
    <p class="about-lead">The NASA Astrophysics Data System went online in 1993, when a scientific website was still a novelty. It has been running ever since, which is why we like to say it has lasted longer than the internet.</p>

    <p>NASA has funded it for thirty-three years. Astronomers on every continent, including Antarctica, treated it as the library they already trusted, and that trust is the thing SciX was most careful not to spend.</p>

    <blockquote class="about-cite">
      the most valuable single contribution to astronomy research the Center for Astrophysics had made in its lifetime
      <footer>2002 Visiting Committee, on the Astrophysics Data System</footer>
    </blockquote>

    <p>When NASA asked for one discovery portal across its science divisions, the team that had spent a quarter century linking astronomy papers to data and software took on Earth science, heliophysics and planetary science as well. The collection grew to about two and a half times the size of the ADS corpus it came from, which meant rebuilding nearly every ingestion and curation process, because each new field arrived with its own publishers, repositories and identifier schemes. Through all of it, every account, saved library and alert kept working, and the researchers who depended on the service never had to do anything about it.</p>
