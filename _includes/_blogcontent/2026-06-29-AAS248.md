The [American Astronomical Society](https://aas.org/) met for the [248th time](https://aas.org/meetings/aas248) in [Pasadena, California](https://www.britannica.com/place/Pasadena-California), from June 14 through 18 along with its [High Energy Astrophysics Division](https://head.aas.org/) and [Laboratory Astrophysics Division](https://lad.aas.org/). The summer meetings tends to be smaller with a slower place, which provide greater opportunities for conversation and building deeper connections. However, because of the lower attendance, {% if include.ads %}ADS{% else %}SciX{% endif %} does not usually have a booth. This year, we just sent project scientist for astrophysics, {% if include.ads %}[Jennifer Lynn Bartlett](https://orcid.org/0000-0001-7394-4545){% else %}[Jennifer Lynn Bartlett](https://scixplorer.org/scixabout/team/team/jbartlett.html){% endif %}, because she has multiple roles within the Society. (And it distracts her from writing abstracts for vintage papers no one else is likely to read.)  
Before the main meeting, the [AAS Strategic Assembly](https://aas.org/about/governance/aas-strategic-assembly) met on June 14 to begin discussions that will lead to a new [five-year strategic plan](https://aas.org/sites/default/files/2021-07/AAS-Strategic-Plan-2021-26.pdf) for the Society.  The [Board of Trustees](https://aas.org/about/governance/current-board) along with the chairs of the divisions, committees, and working groups worked through tough questions about our current priorities and opportunities.  The AAS does many things for the astronomical community and most of them should continue but not all of them are strategic nor should everything receive the same priority, attention, or resources. Given our mission, vision, and values plus our understanding of the units we serve with the Society, what should the AAS be doing for the short- and long-term health of our field and the climate in which we do our science?  These discussions are uncomfortable but astronomy is a human activity and what we do, whether professionally or recreationally, is connected to every other part of our lives and communities. When it is ready, the strategic plan will be shared with the whole society and available to the public. For now, suffice to say many more crucial conversations will be necessary before the framework and top priorities begin to take their final shape. Bartlett is a member of the Strategic Assembly because she is chair of the [Working Group on the Preservation of Astronomical Heritage](https://aas.org/comms/working-group-preservation-astronomical-heritage-wgpah), which relates to the {% if include.ads %}ADS{% else %}SciX{% endif %} [historical literature collection](https://scixplorer.org/search?d=astrophysics&p=1&q=bibgroup%3Ahistoricalliterature&sort=date+asc&sort=date+desc), and vice chair of the [Working Group on the Unified Astronomy Thesaurus](https://aas.org/comms/working-group-unified-astronomy-thesaurus-wguat), which is the {% if include.ads %}ADS{% else %}SciX{% endif %} preferred [keyword system for astronomy, heliophysics, and planetary science](https://astrothesaurus.org/). If you have suggestions regarding the future direction of the Society or profession, please contact Bartlett (jennifer.bartlett@cfa.harvard.edu) or any member of the Strategic Assembly.

For something completely different, Bartlett presented "[Observing Arctic Twilight](https://aas242-aas.ipostersessions.com/?s=79-88-E6-F3-7D-5C-7A-9D-10-54-90-14-80-27-09-9B)" on Monday, June 15 in the evening.  She, [Robert Kerr (CPI)](https://www.linkedin.com/in/bob-kerr-58779162/), [Eduardo Rodriguez-Feo Bermudez (Pacific Northwest National Laboratory)](https://www.linkedin.com/in/eduardo-rodriguez-feo-bermudez-28650b170/), [Charles Poole (CPI)](https://www.linkedin.com/in/charles-poole-58ba551/), [Michael Migliozzi (CPI)](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mike-migliozzi-716b064/), and [James Hilton (0000-0001-9431-4331)](https://orcid.org/0000-0001-9431-4331) describe the all-sky illuminance (spectroradiometric) measurements she collected in the Arctic during September and October 2019. After shelving them for over six years, the [U.S. Naval Observatory](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/) recently public released her data so that they could be analyzed gpt maritime safety and observation planning purposes.  
Thom Burns, curator of the Harvard Plate Stacks, presented "Numbering the Women Who Numbered the Stars" on Tuesday, June 16 in the afternoon. (We are going to blame her for that silly title as well.) He described work begun with Bartlett and [Lisa Hees](https://www.linkedin.com/in/lisahees/), former assistant curator to develop [bibliographies](https://scixplorer.org/public-libraries/jqWiU27iTyuNgy4dd5tnQg), [public libraries](https://scixplorer.org/scixhelp/libraries-scix/creating-libraries), in {% if include.ads %}ADS{% else %}SciX{% endif %} for [Women Astronomical Computers](https://platestacks.cfa.harvard.edu/women-at-hco) and [early women scientists](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:GLAM/Smithsonian_Institution/Funk_List) and have [International Standard Name Identifiers (ISNIs)](https://isni.org/) and [Library of Congress Name Authorities](https://id.loc.gov/authorities/names.html?_) assigned to these authors. This work is exploring how to idenitify unique authors within the {% if include.ads %}ADS{% else %}SciX{% endif %} historical literature for whom [Open Researcher and Contributor IDs (ORCiDs)](https://orcid.org/) are not available. He announced that the [Smithsonian American Women’s History Museum](https://womenshistory.si.edu/) awarded them a one-year grant to support this work and develop the bibliographies into [digital profiles](https://scixplorer.org/scixhelp/libraries-scix/library-example-martha-shapley).
