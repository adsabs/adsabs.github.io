Constructing Constellations of Computers (C3) makes the research of Women Astronomical Computers and other early women scientists easier to find, credit and reuse in ADS and SciX. The project is building Digital Profiles that bring together each woman's publications, name variants and related archival resources, so that researchers and the public can see her work alongside the literature it helped shape.

<h3 style="margin-top: 0; color: #5FBFAE;">Why it matters</h3>

Women carried out much of the measurement, computation and analysis behind early astronomy, but their work is hard to find in today's literature search systems. Many published under both maiden and married names, or under names that changed over their careers. Author searches often match only a last name and first initial, and persistent author identifiers such as ORCID are only available to living researchers. Much of their contribution was never recorded as authorship at all, and survives only in acknowledgments, measurement descriptions and institutional reports.

<h3 style="margin-top: 0; color: #5FBFAE;">What the project is doing</h3>

* **Digital Profiles.** The team is creating Digital Profiles for at least 100 Women Astronomical Computers and early women scientists, starting from published histories including Smith-Zrull's *Women in Glass* (2021), Palmieri's *They Were Astronomers* (2023) and Hanner's work on Margaret Harwood and the Maria Mitchell Observatory. To reflect the disciplines SciX covers, the list also draws on the Funk list of more than 400 notable Smithsonian women scientists and Margaret Rossiter's *Women Scientists in America*. Each profile reconciles name variants, corrects bibliographies and links the right records to the right person.
* **Persistent identifiers.** Where needed, the project creates or strengthens [ISNI](https://isni.org) records for these women, so their work can be identified reliably across systems.
* **Credit beyond authorship.** A natural language processing pipeline will search the scanned historical literature in ADS and SciX for acknowledgments of women's computations, measurements and observations. ADS/SciX librarians and scientists review every candidate before it is linked to a profile. The team will document the pipeline's performance and limitations, including possible gender and historical biases.
* **Connecting the archive.** Profiles build on existing links between ADS/SciX records for Harvard Plate Stacks notebooks, transcribed through the Smithsonian Transcription Center as part of Project PHaEDRA, and the digitized photographic plates in [StarGlass](https://starglass.cfa.harvard.edu).
* **Sharing the method.** Documentation and training materials will let librarians, archivists and other institutions add profiles of the women in their own histories. The code, training data and model will be released openly.

The project runs from summer 2026 through August 2027.
