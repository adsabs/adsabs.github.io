Your donation will fund the digitization of _<a href="https://astrosociety.org/news-publications/mercury-magazine/" target="_blank" referrer="none">Mercury</a>_ magazine, the
<a href="https://astrosociety.org/" target="_blank" referrer="none">Astronomical Society of the Pacific</a>'s popular-level publication. This
effort will digitize the magazine's issues printed from 1972 to 2007 and
make them freely available in the <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/" target="_blank" referrer="none">NASA Astrophysics Data System</a> and
<a href="https://scixplorer.org/" target="_blank" referrer="none">Science Explorer</a>, allowing the entire publication to be openly
accessible online.

<h3 style="margin-top: 0; color: #5FBFAE;">A 35-year gap in the public record of astronomy</h3>

Founded in 1889, the Astronomical Society of the Pacific has spent more
than a century connecting professional astronomers, educators, and the
public. Its magazine _Mercury_ debuted in January 1972 with a clear
purpose: to interpret the results of astronomical research for the
non-specialist. Across the decades that followed, it chronicled the
space age as it unfolded --- the Viking landings, the first exoplanets,
the Hubble era --- in the words of the scientists and science writers
who lived it.

Issues from 2007 forward are online. Everything printed between 1972 and
2007 exists only on paper, scattered across member bookshelves and
library stacks, effectively invisible to anyone searching today.

<h3 style="margin-top: 0; color: #5FBFAE;">Where these issues will live</h3>

The Astrophysics Data System, operated by the Smithsonian Astrophysical
Observatory --- part of the Center for Astrophysics \| Harvard &
Smithsonian --- under a grant from NASA, is the front door to
astronomical literature for researchers worldwide, with bibliographic
information on more than 13 million publications.

Its next-generation platform, Science Explorer (SciX), extends that same
infrastructure across planetary science, heliophysics, and the Earth and
environmental sciences. Placing _Mercury_ in ADS and SciX means every
article becomes full-text searchable, permanently citable, and linked
alongside the research literature it was written to explain --- at no
cost to any reader, anywhere.

<h3 style="margin-top: 0; color: #5FBFAE;">What your gift makes possible</h3>

- **Thirty-five years of issues preserved.** Every printed volume from
  1972 through 2007, scanned and archived to modern standards.
- **Free and open access, permanently.** No subscription, no paywall,
  no institutional affiliation required.
- **Discoverable by search.** Full-text indexing places these articles
  in front of the researchers, students, and readers looking for them.
- **A resource for teaching and history.** Educators, historians of
  science, and journalists gain a primary record of how discoveries
  were explained to the public as they happened.
