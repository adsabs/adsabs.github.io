<p class="about-lead">SciX grew out of the NASA Astrophysics Data System. ADS has served astronomers since the early 1990s and is credited with raising the research efficiency of the field by at least <a href="https://scixplorer.org/abs/2005JASIS..56...36K/abstract">six percent</a>, which is roughly the entire annual astronomy output of a G7 country.</p>

<h3 class="about-group-title">Why ADS was the starting point</h3>

<p>ADS went online in 1993, when a scientific website was still a novelty. It has been running ever since, which is why we like to say it has lasted longer than the internet. By the time NASA went looking for a portal that could cover all of its science divisions, ADS had already spent a quarter century doing the hard part: linking papers to the data, software and observations behind them, and keeping those links correct as publishers, archives and identifiers changed underneath.</p>

<p>That groundwork is what made the expansion possible. Heliophysics and planetary science came first, in 2021. Earth science followed in 2022 and 2023. Once "astrophysics" no longer described what was in the collections, the program took the name Science Explorer.</p>

<h3 class="about-group-title">What the expansion involved</h3>

<p>Growing the corpus to more than 36 million records, about two and a half times its former size, meant rebuilding nearly every ingestion and curation process and inventing several new ones. Each new field arrived with its own publishers, its own repositories, its own identifier schemes and its own conventions for what counts as a citable object.</p>

<p>The <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/scixblog/scix-launch">September 2025 launch</a> shipped as a complete service. Every account, saved library and alert from ADS carried over without the user doing anything. There is also a <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sgJ-LolRLu8">video introduction</a> to the platform.</p>

<h3 class="about-group-title">What stayed the same</h3>

<p>SciX is still free, still open source (<a href="https://github.com/adsabs">code</a>, <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/scixblog/ads-models-and-datasets">models and datasets</a>), and still built on the <a href="https://www.go-fair.org/fair-principles/">FAIR principles</a>. ADS remains available and will fold into SciX by 2027.</p>
